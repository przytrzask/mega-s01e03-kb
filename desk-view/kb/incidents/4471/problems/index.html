parser: the shared parser reads dates month first

A day-first tenant’s date comes out with day and month swapped: “Booked for 2 December. The invite says 12 February.” This is what #4471 describes. It covers every report from a day-first tenant that came in by app or email, and form reports from tenants on booking-v2. Fix: parse in the tenant’s locale.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells day-first people to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People follow the hint, and the form reads what they typed the tenant’s way, day first. The swap looks the same as parser, but it comes from the form, so the parser fix does not touch it. It covers form reports from the day-first tenants on booking-v3. Fix: print the hint in the tenant’s order, and read the field in that same order.

one-day: bookings move by exactly one day

“Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” A day–month swap cannot do this: it moves a date by months. These point at a time zone or day-boundary fault: a date taken at midnight in one zone and shown in another. The desk had already read them as one-day moves (desk-notes.json) and footnoted them under parser; here they are a problem of their own. Fix: store a booking as a calendar date plus the tenant’s time zone, not as a midnight timestamp.

How each report is placed

  1. Count each report once: the export repeats some ids.
  2. If the desk read it as a one-day move, it is one-day.
  3. Otherwise causes.ts decides by tenant, not by intake’s tag: a form report from a day-first tenant on booking-v3 is hint; any other report from a day-first tenant is parser.

The logic is in #lib/kb/problems.ts, and the table runs it at render time, so the counts follow the data as the desk keeps merging.

Why #4471 held all three

Decision 0001 merges on subject alone, and all three problems arrive with the same subjects: “Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”. Intake’s tag guesses from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant’s form often. Read what a report shows before you merge it.
